# M95 — A new, harder puzzle: The Midsummer Buffet

You said the hint ladder felt like working things out, and asked for something completely new and more complex because you already know the stage 1.4 chains. This is the first puzzle built on the research report (`docs/design/puzzle-solving-research.md`). Play it from the level list: **P1. The Midsummer Buffet** (series "Puzzles (new)").

## The puzzle (no spoilers beyond what the game tells you)
A golden fish pie stands on a buffet table. Three people mind it. The goal is to get the pie to a picnic blanket. The note shows only wishes ("The chef should have somewhere else to be", "The maître should stop pacing"), then riddles, then the map markers, then the answer, on the M94 hint ladder (J asks for more).

## What is new in the engine
- **Several ways per step** (`ObjectiveDef.events`): any one of a list of events finishes a step, and out-of-order steps are fine (only the carry waits for the pie). The first two steps here accept three and two ways.
- **Watchers** (`NpcSpawn.watch`): somebody who minds a prop notices when it is gone **and they can see the gull** (sight cone, not distracted or busy), and gives chase (`caught:<role>`). A noise, music, a bell, a slip, or a turned back all stop it, and they are derived from the existing senses, not scripted per level.
- **Attention gauge:** a dot over the head of anybody who is growing suspicious of the gull; it swells and goes from yellow to orange to red before the "!" appears. This makes watching the watchers a skill.
- **Per-person events:** `distract:<role>` (any time somebody looks away, not only when holding something) and `dancing:<role>`, so a step can say who.
- Tests: `tests/unit/buffet.test.ts`, `tests/smoke/buffet.spec.ts`.

## For the designer's eyes only: the ways (not shown to the player)
Chef: the service bell sends him off; a clattering bottle or the jukebox (within 20 m) looks him away or makes him dance. Maître: a splat on his route makes him slip; a taunt (T) makes him follow you off the route; the jukebox; a bottle. Granny at the blanket: a bottle, or speed. Honk and the chase system are all still there to improvise with.

## What I would like to learn from you
- Did you find more than one way for each person? Did the gauge help you read them?
- Was the carry past granny fun, or a chore? Did being caught feel funny, or unfair?
- Where did you wait too long before pressing J?
