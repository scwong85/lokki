# M96 — The Buffet, readable

Playtest of M95: (1) hard to tell who the chef is and what the item is, (2) the blanket had no visual cue, (3) no consequence of getting caught, (4) J pressed twice for the blanket step. "There needs to be recognisable item and human for better interaction."

## Changes
- **People you can tell apart:** the Chef has a tall white toque, the Maître a top hat, Granny a lilac sun hat, and each has a **name tag** floating over the head (`NpcSpawn.name`, `hat: 'chef'`). The step text names them ("The Chef (white hat)...", "The Maître (top hat, pacing)...").
- **A pie that looks like a pie:** a real prop (`pie`: golden crust, lattice, a fish tail sticking out, like a kalakukko), no longer a plain fish.
- **Scenery** (`LevelDef.decor`, `src/render/decor.ts`): a **buffet table** with a cloth, plates and buns (solid top, so the pie rests on it) and a **red-checked picnic blanket** with a basket and a pink flag. The blanket moved onto open paving to the west (it was in the tents, hidden). The pie and the blanket show their map marker from the start (`showMarker`): they are things, not methods; the people and how-to stay on the ladder. The blanket step now says which blanket and where.
- **Consequences of being caught:** a watcher who has chased the gull down and reaches it (1.8 m) while it carries the pie takes the pie **straight back to the table**, throws the gull back and dazes it (about 1.4 s), shows a laugh, and says a line ("Not my pie!"). Then they go back to minding things (`tuning.watch`). A chase you outrun still costs nothing, so speed is a way too.
- Tests: the catch (`tests/smoke/buffet.spec.ts`, real wiring), the pie prop and decor in the level data.
