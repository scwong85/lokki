# M94 — The hint ladder trial

From the research report (`docs/design/puzzle-solving-research.md`, changes 1, 2 and 10 in part): the to-do note handed over the whole solution at second zero, so players executed instead of working things out. This trial changes that on three chains and measures nothing but feel.

## Built
- **The ladder** (`src/gameplay/hintLadder.ts`): a step with a `nudge` shows only its **outcome** (the wish, no method). With no progress it adds, in turn, a **cryptic line** (after 25 s), then its **marker on the map** (55 s), then the **full hint** and the explicit text (90 s). `tuning.hints` holds the timings. The clock belongs to each step and stops while the step is not the one being worked on.
- **Ask for more:** J, or the **?** on the note, gives the next rung at once.
- **Options → Hints:** *gradual* (default), *all at once* (the old behaviour) or *only when I ask*.
- **Data:** `ObjectiveDef.outcome` and `.nudge`; steps without a nudge behave exactly as before. `activeMarkers` keeps a ladder step's marker back until rung 2.
- **The trial chains** (all in stage 1.4, and in their Playground levels): Map and Cone, Wet Floor, Jukebox Hero, all their steps. Example, Map and Cone: *"The tourist's map should go missing."* → *"Birds take things from people who are not looking at them."* → the marker → *"The tourist at the east end of the quay is holding a map. Dive on it..."*
- Tests: `tests/unit/hintLadder.test.ts` (rungs, asking, modes, markers, and a rule that outcomes and nudges contain no keys or verbs like hold/press/aim) and `tests/smoke/hint-ladder.spec.ts`.

## What to look for when you play stage 1.4
- Does solving feel different with the wish only? Do you get stuck (and how long before you press J)?
- Is the cryptic line too cryptic or too easy? Do the timings feel right?
- Does the chain feel less like a walkthrough, or does it just feel like missing information? (That would point to the second half of the report: several ways to solve each step, and attention you can read.)

## Not done (the rest of the report's plan)
Several events per step, an attention gauge over heads, routines for the puzzle cast, fewer waiting/fetch steps, witness reactions derived from sight, finales as route problems, toy-first stages.
