# M124 — One more row in every Act 1 stage

Asked after the first playtests of the ten-minute stages ("for each stage add one additional row to extend the time"). Each new row uses a trick the stage does not use yet, is far from the roof and from the other rows, and sits in the last course.

| Stage | New row | Where | The trick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 First Light | **The Lost Tourist's map** | far north, pacing a 36 m beat | a **moving target**: dive at a map held by someone who never stands still, outrun him, carry it home |
| 1.2 The Gull Picnic | **The Pie Man's pie** (Dessert, now 3 rows) | far north | **poop** from above: a guarded holder panics and his pie is open for a few seconds; the Guard whistles at it like the rest |
| 1.3 Market Day Heist | **The Watchman's sausage** (getaway, now 3 rows) | north-west | a **fire alarm** 13 m from his post: the noise brings him over to look; take the sausage meanwhile |
| 1.4 One Bad Afternoon | **The Peekers' cone** (good vibrations, now 3 rows) | far north-west | **hide in the bush and BOO** (the verb of 1.3, used again) |
| 1.5 The Warden | **The Domino Queue's cone** (lunch break, now 2 rows) | north-west | **dominoes**: peck the first tile, the row falls into the queue, the queue goes over like skittles |

Also: gongs say the new count ("Seven things on the blanket", "Table for seven"), the courses' "where to look" lines mention the new places, guest gulls match the row count, par times follow (1.1 8:00, 1.2 10:00, 1.3 7:00, 1.4 11:00, 1.5 12:00 for a practised run; first plays will take longer), the Guard confiscates the pie.

Tests: unit tests for rows, courses, spread (55 m from the roof, 35 m between rows; the vendor corner and the boss excepted), land, tags and the pie's splat-then-open rule; browser tests walk every new row (the Lost Tourist paces, the Pie Man's pie is served, the alarm brings the Watchman over, the bush honk makes the Peekers drop their cones, the first tile topples the queue). The real dive on the moving map and the real splat on the Pie Man were not replayed by hand.
