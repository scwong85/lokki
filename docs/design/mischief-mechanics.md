# Mischief mechanics: a catalogue to diversify the levels

Status: **study and plan. Two mechanics built (M48: Slip, Pied Piper)**. Round 2 (section 6 onwards) widens the study to NPC and environment interaction and to new gull verbs. **Built from round 2 (M49): Honk (V1) and Cannonball (V6).** Tug (V2) was built in M52.

Written from the playtest note: "the levels are too similar, the mechanics are the same. Diversify the play style; study different fun mechanics that make it fun and funny to be mischievous."

## 1. What the research says

Sources: [Game Developer, Road to the IGF: Untitled Goose Game](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-house-house-s-i-untitled-goose-game-i-), [an Untitled Goose Game analysis](https://www.jikguard.com/news/an-untitled-untitled-goose-game-analysis), [Kill Screen on funny games](https://www.killscreen.com/year-games-got-funny-again), [Octodad post-mortem, design](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/octodad-dadliest-catch-post-mortem-pt-3-design), [Goat Simulator coverage](https://godisageek.com/2014/04/goat-simulator-review/).

1. **Few verbs, many reactions.** Goose Game has about three and a half verbs (honk, run, grab, flap) and the comedy comes from how *people* react to them. New content should add reactions and toys, and only rarely a verb.
2. **Open-ended puzzles.** Almost everything is tactile and most puzzles can be solved several ways; the small interactions between tasks carry the fun. A step that has only one solution reads as a chore.
3. **Slapstick needs a telegraph and a payoff.** The joke is the *setup you can see coming* (the puddle, the line of people behind you) and then the physical consequence. Present the setup, let the player cause it, show the fall.
4. **Introduce one thing at a time** (Octodad's lesson): the player has to learn the toy well enough to fail at it funnily.
5. **Comedy has a shelf life** (Goat Simulator runs out of ideas after a few hours): variety of *toys* matters more than depth in any one of them.
6. **Low stakes, no fail states**, restraint, and a funny walk.

### What that means for Lokki
- **Variety rule:** no two neighbouring levels share their *core toy*. A level has one new or featured toy plus the stock reactions (snatch, splat, taunt, noise).
- **Chains are the how, toys are the what.** Chain steps (cause and reaction) stay; each level picks a different *toy* to drive them.
- **At least two ways per step** where possible, and **no precision**: big zones, forgiving timings, no landing on small things.

## 4. How the two built toys work (for reference)

- **Slip (T1).** Every splat on the ground leaves a wet patch for 40 s (radius 1.7 m); a free prop tagged `slippery` (a wet fish) is a puddle too. A walking person who steps in one slips: arms up, a long slide, whatever they hold flies out of their hand, and they are angrier (a second slip makes them furious). `NpcSpawn.patrol` makes people pace a route, so a trap can be set and waited on. Wet Floor: splat the route, watch him slip, take his lunch.
- **Pied Piper (T2).** `NpcSpawn.follows` marks people who fall in behind a taunting gull within 22 m and keep following for 6 s after; they queue at 1.2 m gaps nearest-first. A level's `parade` lists stops that fire an event (once) when enough followers are in a place, and can send them somewhere (the wrong ferry).

# Round 2: more NPC and environment interaction, and new gull verbs

## 6. What more research says

Sources: [Goeland: Seagull Adventure coverage](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/goeland-is-like-untitled-goose-game-but-youre-a-seagull-in-a-town-thats-a-bit-too-peaceful/page=1/) and [its press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/spread-your-wings-and-sow-carnage-in-goeland-seagull-adventure-), [Catlateral Damage](https://igf.com/catlateral-damage), [Immersive sims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersive_sim), [Hitman NPC reaction research (fan forum, treat as observation)](https://www.hitmanforum.com/t/research-npc-reactions/16088), [Untitled Goose Game interviews](https://www.gamesradar.com/untitled-goose-game-preview), [a review on how the joke wears thin](https://www.bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/untitled-goose-game-review/1), [The Occupation's NPC smart objects](https://www.vice.com/en/article/a-thriller-where-the-ai-gets-stressed-drinks-too-much-and-needs-to-pee).

1. **Noise, light and sight are systems, not scripts** (Thief, Hitman). A thrown object makes a noise that any NPC in earshot reacts to; the same noise works with any item. Lokki already has noise; it has no *light*.
2. **NPC states are shared vocabulary**: idle, suspicious, investigating, alerted, chasing, panicking. Witnesses **panic when they see someone panic** (Hitman). Lokki's moods are the right base; the missing piece is *contagion*.
3. **Hiding places are a verb** (Hitman crates, cupboards): somewhere to be safe to scout and plan from. The gull has no hiding verb.
4. **Disguise is stronger from behind** (Hitman 2: enforcers are less perceptive when you face away). Lokki's Disguise skill could use the same rule.
5. **Mini-games on top of the base verbs** (Goeland: tug-of-war for a hotdog, tic-tac-toe with a nasty surprise; a reactive crowd; a town that changes with your actions). Snatching today is instant; a **tug-of-war** gives the snatch a physical, funny middle.
6. **Knock it over** (Catlateral Damage): the pure joy of the push. Lokki's tipping exists but is rarely the point.
7. **Smart objects** (The Occupation): NPCs use things (sit, queue, buy, take photos), so things have *uses* that can be disrupted.
8. **Warning from the reviews**: simple distractions run out fast. Depth comes from a **second order of consequence** (what the victim does next to someone else), which is why Slip and Pied Piper work: the effect travels from one person to the next.

# Round 3: decisions and the Tug design

Answers from the owner after round 2: (1) Honk on a tap of T is fine. (2) **Tug works alongside the snatch, not instead of it** (below). (3) Start with the cheap pair: **Honk and Cannonball** (built in M49). (4) **Environment sounds were missing** (tram, church bell): fixed in M49.

## 12. Tug design: three ways to take an item

The snatch and the tug are two verbs with different strengths; each *item* says which ones work on it.

| | **Snatch** (from the air) | **Tug** (from the ground) |
|---|---|---|
| How | Dive or swoop at the item, press E at the right moment (today's snatch) | Walk up, hold E on the item in their hand; a struggle starts |
| Strength | Fast, safe from sprays and from the vendor, but needs a guard gap (they must not be watching) or a distraction | Works on someone who *is* watching, and on things a swoop cannot reach (low, held close to the body) |
| Risk | Missing the window | **The struggle takes seconds, and anything that notices you can interrupt it**: a vendor's spray soaks you and you lose; the Warden arrives; the owner pulls harder if they are angry |
| Co-op | One taunts, one snatches | One taunts or honks to shorten the struggle, or both tug and it is won at once |

Each item gets `grab: 'air' | 'ground' | 'both'`:
- **air only**: things a person holds high or over the head, or fragile things that would tear in a tug (a balloon, a flag, a cap on a tall man, a held-up phone).
- **ground only**: things held low or tight to the body (a handbag, a tray, a hotdog clutched in two hands, the vendor's cash box), or things guarded by a person who never lets up (the Warden's sandwich).
- **both**: the common case (ice cream, bun, map).

The struggle itself (no precision, no new button, E hold):
1. Hold E near their hand: a meter fills while you hold; their pull drains it (stronger when they are angry, weaker when startled or taunted).
2. **Flap** (Space) gives a burst, so the gull "pulls with its wings".
3. Win: it is yours. Lose (meter empty or interrupted): they keep it and you get knocked back with a smug emote.
4. **Interruptions**: a spray cone from a vendor, a honk from another gull (they flinch and let go: a *win* for you), the owner's friend arriving, the dog barking.
5. Because it needs you on the ground next to a person, **it is the louder, riskier way**: heat rises more than for a clean snatch, and the person remembers.

Effort M. It builds on the snatch code, guard states and the vendor spray cone, all of which exist.

## 13. Built in M49
- **Honk**: tap T (quick press); calm people within 14 m jump; someone with their back turned drops what they hold; sleepers within about 8 m wake; the pigeons on the statue take off. Holding T is still the taunt (it starts after 0.25 s). Level: **M3. Honk!**
- **Cannonball**: a hard dive into the water (faster than 9 m/s) makes a wave that soaks everyone within 3 to 8 m (by speed): they drop what they hold and run. Level: **M4. Splash Landing**.
- **Sound**: a real church bell (low strike with partials that ring on for seconds, heard at any distance but quieter far away), a tram bell (ding-ding, fading with distance up to 160 m), a ferry horn (finale), and always-on layers: the tram's rumble, the sea when you are near water, and the city murmur when people are near. The old bell and tram sounds were a tiny 880 Hz blip at one volume.

## 14. Built in M52: Tug of war
- **Air snatch, ground tug.** From the air (or a dive) the snatch is instant, as before. On the ground, pressing E next to someone's hand starts a **tug of war**: a meter (the orange bar over the HUD) fills while you hold E (slowly) and jumps with every press (mash E, or tap the Grab button on a phone: a tap keeps it going for 0.4 s). They pull back by how they feel: a calm person 0.12, someone on guard 0.40, angry +0.25, startled by a honk 0.02, distracted by a noise 30 % of that. Win: it is yours (and they chase you as with any snatch). Lose: they keep it, hang on, and watch you.
- **Items say how they can be taken** (`PropDef.grab`): *air only* (balloon, student cap, top hat, scarf: held high or loose), *ground only* (soup bowl, raw herring: held in both hands, a swoop cannot take them), and *both* (everything else, as before).
- **A vendor's spray breaks a tug** and you lose it (the ground way is the risky way near a vendor). A honk makes the person let go almost at once. A noise or a taunt weakens their pull.
- Level **M5. Tug of War** (Mischief): the soup (tug), the balloon (dive), the bun (either way, but a vendor stands next door).
- Not done: a partner who joins the tug (co-op), heat for tugging (it is noisy: they notice), per-item tug strengths, and guests hold E only by mashing (their held key is not sent to the host).
