# Lokki hands over answers instead of questions

Lokki's "no sense of puzzle solving" is most likely a presentation and structure problem, not a shortage of mechanics. The repo audit found that every chain step shows its verb, key, target and outcome from second zero, so the player has the whole answer once they have read the current step. What remains is execution (aim and timing), which is not the same as solving. The evidence on Untitled Goose Game points the other way. Its to-do list states an outcome and leaves the method open, and its NPCs have a few discrete, readable attention states. Its best moments are visible NPC reactions rather than ticked boxes, and it recombines earlier tools in its finale. The Lokki systems (sight cone, hearing, suspicion, moods, contagion, gossip, stations, crumbs) can already support that style. The priority changes are therefore: (1) turn the to-do hint from an always-on recipe into a delayed ladder; (2) accept several events per step and write steps as outcomes; (3) make NPC attention and routines visible and observable; (4) cut passive and fetch-terminal steps; (5) rebuild the finales as route problems. **Evidence caveat:** several sites were unreachable, so nearly all external claims below come from search-result summaries, not full pages. Section 1 says how much weight each claim can bear. Lokki's own facts are read directly from the repo (the audit read files but did not run the game).

## What the sources support, and how firmly

Three evidence grades are used here. **Repo-verified** means the audit read the file. **Source-reported** means a search summary relayed a claim and a URL is given, but the page was not read in full. **Inference** is my reasoning and is labelled as such. Developer statements about Goose Game are all source-reported. Disseldorp said the team had no detailed plan up front and that many ideas appear as to-do items ([Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-honk-an-i-untitled-goose-game-i-q-a)). Puzzles with one-off conceits were drawn from "a big list of gags that we wrote up early on", though the outlet for that exact quote is unconfirmed ([Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-house-house-s-i-untitled-goose-game-i-)). Strasser wanted challenges that felt less like contraptions players "butt their heads against" until an aha moment, calling them "social lubrication" ([Tumblr interview summary](https://gaming.tumblr.com/post/187842058055/indie-game-spotlight-untitled-goose-game-oh)). The design started from the goose rather than from puzzles ([Vice interview](https://www.vice.com/en/article/honks-vs-quacks-a-long-chat-with-the-developers-of-untitled-goose-game/)). Nothing retrievable covers House House's playtest method or how they iterated readability, so any claim about "how they tested it" is unsupported. Single-source critic claims are marked as opinion throughout.

The recurring critical picture is consistent enough to use. The verbs are few: run, flap, honk and grab ([The Sixth Axis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2019/09/22/untitled-goose-game-review-switch-pc/)). The list can be finished in nearly any order, with many entries as puzzles or scavenger hunts ([Well-Played](https://www.well-played.com.au/untitled-goose-game-review/)). Puzzles are about working out how townspeople respond, harder ones need chain reactions and moving NPCs, and many have several solutions ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/us/games/283441/untitled-goose-game-review), [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/untitled-goose-game-review-pc)). The team found stealing when people were not looking funnier than running up and taking things, and borrowed stealth tropes of very limited vision and very discrete AI states for noticing something missing or spotting the goose ([Red Bull interview](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/untitled-goose-game-interview)). Failing is cheap: one reviewer notes the game has little fail-state, so players are free to experiment ([Super Jump](https://medium.com/super-jump/untitled-untitled-goose-game-piece-3282b593a0c4)).

## Goose Game hooks the player by naming outcomes and showing reactions

The to-do list is the central device, and it works by asking a question rather than prescribing steps. Trailer examples included "rake in the lake" and "get the groundskeeper wet" ([PC Gamer via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/pc-gamer-us/20171114/284412739365265)). A GDC 2020 narrative review argues the list reveals the goose's personality and gates progression until the player commits to tasks ([Arroz paper](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/GameNarrativeReview/MaryArroz-UntitledGooseGame-paper.pdf)). **Inference:** these are short comic imperatives that name an outcome, not a method, so the goal is the question and the toolset is the answer. The sources do not state this as a rule; it is the pattern in the examples. The same applies to the optional layer: a timed "Quickly!!" list and 15 hidden "to-do (as well)" tasks, each tied to a Steam achievement, sit after the credits ([Nintendo Life walkthrough](https://nintendolife.com/news/2020/05/guide_untitled_goose_game_walkthrough_-_puzzle_solutions_and_to-do_list_objectives), [PowerPyx](https://www.powerpyx.com/untitled-goose-game-trophy-guide-roadmap/)).

NPCs supply the puzzle, and the payoff is their reaction. Disseldorp said the groundskeeper's responses are often what makes the goose seem so bad ([PC Gamer via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/pc-gamer-us/20171114/284412739365265)). The game tracks a villager "interest level" toward the goose, and the score reacts: silent while the goose mulls, low energy while it plans, active when chased ([triple j](https://www.abc.net.au/triplej/programs/triplej-breakfast/untitled-goose-game-composer-dan-golding-interactive-music-chat/12721892)). Critics' favourite moments follow the same shape. Gizmodo found the strongest beats came when townspeople noticed, feared and even applauded the goose ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/a-birdwatchers-review-of-untitled-goose-game-1838402227)). A reviewer's favourite scene was sneaking up behind two people at the pub and honking (outlet attribution unverified, [Gameluster](https://gameluster.com/untitled-goose-game-review/)), and another writer's favourite moment came right after a failed prank ([Into Indie Games](https://intoindiegames.com/reviews/untitled-goose-game-review/), attribution unverified). The finale gets particular praise: AV Club says carrying the golden bell back through the whole map with the tools and routes the player already knows is "a wonderful twist on the rules" ([AV Club](https://avclub.com/with-its-final-puzzle-untitled-goose-game-becomes-a-ma-1838423723)). **Inference, flagged as such:** the shared shape is a visible goal, an NPC with a legible attention state, and a visible reaction as the payoff.

Goose Game is not a flawless model, and the criticisms matter for Lokki. Reviewers call objectives "loose and sometimes a little cryptic" ([The Corkman](https://www.pressreader.com/ireland/the-corkman/20191003/283223034876769)). One says waddling around unable to complete a task is frustrating ([Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/51756)). Another reports a difficulty spike in the final objective ([Pixelpoppers](https://pixelpoppers.com/review/untitled-goose-game/)). Puzzle quality is called mixed ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=242748)), some fetch objectives repeat ([The Outer Haven](https://www.theouterhaven.net/2019/09/untitled-goose-game-review-honkin-around)), and the camera is the most consistent technical complaint. Lokki should therefore copy the outcome-first, reaction-first approach but not the cryptic extreme without a safety net, which is what a hint ladder provides.

## Comparable games and puzzle theory agree: few verbs, interacting rules, dial-down help

Hitman is the closest precedent for guidance that can be dialled down. Its Opportunities and Mission Stories reveal that a route exists and its first steps, and Hitman 2 added assistance levels of Full, Minimal and Off ([Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/hitman-2/), [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/hitman-2-review/), via summaries). Critics faulted the 2016 game for excessive hand-holding ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/reviews/hitman-season-1-review/)), and IO described guided stories as breadcrumbs meant to plant seeds of alternate routes ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/hitman-3-interview-the-dna-of-an-io-game-and-what-it-means-for-the-future-of-hitman-and-bond/)). The Zelda chemistry engine is the model for rules over scripts. Fujibayashi described a shift from "additive" to "multiplicative" design, where objects react to the player and to each other ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-17-breath-of-the-wild-science-lies)), and Dohta put it as creating a system that makes fun things happen rather than something fun ([Thumbsticks GDC17](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc17-designing-zelda-breath-of-the-wild/)). Donut County's Esposito wanted each puzzle specific to its place and resident ([Indie Games Plus](https://indiegamesplus.com/donut-county-developer-talks-about-the-nunances-of-hole-based-play/)). Octodad is the cautionary tale: later stealth sections where enemies spot you almost at once drew criticism ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/octodad-dadliest-catch-the-kotaku-review-1512040710)). Evidence on the weaker seagull clones is thin: itch.io player comments cite unclear goals and too little to interact with ([FreeGull '98 comments](https://haring.itch.io/freegull-98/comments)), but there are no critic reviews, so the claim that clones "fall short" rests on hobby-game comments.

Puzzle theory supports the same direction, with limits. Blow's framing is that a puzzle communicates one idea and that the solving process is the message ([GMTK transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/LaGKzzSXnuQl/en-gb/1/download/How%20Jonathan%20Blow%20Designs%20a%20Puzzle%20%20Game%20Makers%20Toolkit.en-gb.txt)). Immersive-sim practice sets goals without enforcing means and gives every situation several paths ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersive_sim)). Weird West advice is to justify locked-off solutions diegetically ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-weird-wests-developers-put-the-immersive-in-immersive-sim-)). Hint-system guidance recommends optional, escalating hints of 3 to 5 levels, with a 10 to 15 second inactivity trigger as a starting point to calibrate by playtest ([Adventure Game Designer Handbook](https://brycecovert.github.io/adventure-game-designer-handbook/docs/faq.html), a rule of thumb, not research). A University of Washington study reports 66.3% of users felt hint systems met their needs poorly because of timing and presentation ([Hao et al. 2022](https://faculty.washington.edu/weicaics/paper/papers/HaoHZC2022.pdf)). Insight research says aha moments cannot be rushed and incubation helps ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/between-cultures/202510/8-insights-into-aha-moments)), and self-determination theory links game enjoyment to autonomy and competence ([Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2009_PrzbylskiRyanRigby_PSPB-1.pdf)). **Not found:** any source on cryptic versus explicit to-do wording, on Rube Goldberg chain design, on "toys before puzzles" by name, or on humour amplifying insight. Those links in my argument are inference.

## The repo audit shows a walkthrough with a rails-bound world

All findings in this section are repo-verified by reading, not by playing. The to-do note renders the first open step's `hint` unconditionally ([src/ui/todo.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/ui/todo.ts)). The hints give the method. Map and Cone says "Hold T to taunt so he keeps following, and fly low towards the ice cream stall (the south row, in the middle)"; Wet Floor says "Aim a poop (F) at the ground inside the ring"; Breakfast quotes the author's own insight, "when she counts 3, 2, 1, dive on the 1" ([src/data/levels/mapAndCone.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/mapAndCone.ts), [src/data/levels/mischief.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/mischief.ts), [src/data/levels/arc1.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/arc1.ts)). Quest markers name the actor or place, and roughly 45% of steps carry one ([src/gameplay/questMarkers.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/gameplay/questMarkers.ts), approximate count). Wanted items glow from 40 m ([src/gameplay/itemGlow.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/gameplay/itemGlow.ts)). Stage intros add a verb tip, and a 75-second idle nudge (M83) re-shows a hint that is already on screen. The author's own doc says the ladder should be "note text, then marker, then the world demonstrates it" ([docs/design/puzzle-quality.md](/home/user/lokki/docs/design/puzzle-quality.md)); the build runs all rungs at once. The joke card's `insight` field is author-only data, and the player sees it as instruction.

Structurally, all 30 solo chains in stages 1.1 to 2.5 are linear sequences with one accepted event string per step and `after:` gating ([src/gameplay/quest.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/gameplay/quest.ts)). The chain-levels doc asks for "two or more ways to cause each step" ([docs/design/chain-levels.md](/home/user/lokki/docs/design/chain-levels.md)), but the data does not reflect it. The audit's own rough counts: about 3 chains turn on a real "what does this person do if I...?" experiment (Map and Cone, Jukebox Hero, Bottle Service), about 12 are "verb at marked actor, then take the dropped item", and about 6 contain a pure wait-for-timer step. About a third of steps are passive consequences, and 20-plus chains end on a `grab:` item payoff. Four chain shapes recur (verb then grab, wait for window then snatch, the anger-station-buy loop, a trap on a patrol route), so after stage 1.2 the player meets no new kind of question. Consequences are mostly authored per event: on `bunAtDock` a dockhand is sent to a fixed goal and made oblivious for 14 seconds. Playtest fixes M85, M86, M88 and M92 repeatedly made steps fire more reliably or hints more explicit, which is the right fix for a bug and the wrong direction for discovery. Finally, the quality test checks that joke, insight and reaction strings exist and that a regex-matched "reaction" appears, but that regex counts the player's own actions (`snatch`, `robbed`, `pecked`, `pose`), so rules pass on form while intent fails ([tests/unit/puzzle.test.ts](/home/user/lokki/tests/unit/puzzle.test.ts)).

The brain is the strength to build on. NPC states include notice, shield, panic, chase and dance, with moods, sight cones blocked by geometry, suspicion that doubles under dive and gossip that makes people wary for 90 seconds ([src/gameplay/npc.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/gameplay/npc.ts)). Legibility is the weaker half. The audit found no suspicion indicator in render or UI code (a grep-absence claim), so players follow the red or green guard ring rather than reading attention. Most puzzle cast are stationary (about 26 per living-world.md), so "watch what they do" yields nothing. Slow motion and the lingering camera were removed in M87 because they did not feel right ([docs/milestones/M83.md](/home/user/lokki/docs/milestones/M83.md)), leaving the accomplishment beat as banner, polaroid and stamp.

## Prioritised changes and proposed milestones

**Inference throughout:** the sources justify the direction (outcome-first goals, dial-down help, legible discrete states, reaction payoffs, recombination finales); the specific designs and numbers below are my proposals and need playtesting. The milestone numbers assume M92 is the latest, per the audit; renumber as needed.

| Priority | Change | Grounding | Proposed milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hint ladder replaces always-on hints: note shows the outcome only; after ~15 s of no progress a cryptic line; then a world cue; the explicit hint only on request. Options: Hints Full / Minimal / Off. | Hitman assistance levels; handbook 10-15 s starting point; own puzzle-quality.md | M93 Hint ladder |
| 2 | Rewrite step text as outcomes ("The photographer is too happy. Fix that."); remove method, key and target from `text`; keep the explicit version as the last rung. | Goose Game to-do examples; Weird West | M93 |
| 3 | Multi-event steps: a step accepts a set of events (honk, poop, toss all count as distraction); `after:` stops blocking out-of-order progress (banked). | Den of Geek, Prima (multiple solutions); chain-levels.md s.2 | M94 Many ways |
| 4 | Make attention legible: a small attention gauge or posture lean above the head that fills with suspicion (gradient curious to alarmed), and a distinct reaction sound per state. | Stealth-indicator practice; Goose Game discrete states and interest level | M95 Readable people |
| 5 | Give people watchable routines: move key puzzle cast (photographer, dockhand, vendors) onto 3-stop routines already built for strollers (M89), and remove the route and timing from hints so the player has to watch. | Audit gap 7; Goose Game moving-NPC puzzles | M95 |
| 6 | Cut passive and fetch-terminal steps: merge wait and consequence steps into the action that causes them; end some chains on the reaction (the person's visible meltdown or a crowd laugh) rather than a stolen cone. | Audit gaps 4 and 5; Braid "no padding" | M96 Fewer chores |
| 7 | Reaction-first feedback: derive witness reactions from who actually saw (radius and sight cone) instead of fixed coordinates; a brief camera hold or a replay polaroid on the payoff. Test any camera beat carefully, since slow motion was removed for feeling wrong. | Goose Game reactions as payoff; audit s.3 | M97 Payoff beats |
| 8 | Rebuild finales as route problems that recombine earlier verbs through the map: Nemesis (fountain, queue, slip) and Key to the City as a single carry-through, not a gated checklist. | AV Club on the golden-bell finale; puzzle-quality.md s.4 promise | M98 and M99 Finales |
| 9 | Toy-first stages: open each stage with a short goal-less sandbox patch for the new verb before the chain, and extend crumbs, boom box and shoo into the campaign. | Gage sandbox-first (source-reported); audit s.4 | with M96 |
| 10 | Replace the formal quality test with measures that matter: accepted ways per step, steps with no hint, timer steps, steps whose "reaction" is the player's own action. | Audit s.6; test regex | M93 |

The ordering is by return on effort. Priorities 1 and 2 are mostly data and one UI file, and attack the highest-ranked diagnosis. Priority 3 is the only engine change on the critical path and unlocks everything after it. Priorities 4 and 5 make the brain visible, which supports the ladder's last rung ("the world demonstrates it"). One risk is worth stating: removing hints raises the stuck-player risk that Goose Game reviewers also report, and Hitman's hand-holding backlash shows the opposite risk. That is why the ladder and the Hints setting come first, before any content is made harder. Priority 8 should ship last because it depends on everything else.

## Conclusion

The playtester's complaint most likely follows from a consistent trade made in earlier milestones: each playtest bug was fixed by making the chain more explicit and more scripted, which raised reliability and lowered discovery. Lokki's difficulty is currently execution, and execution is the part of the experience that testers do not call puzzle solving. The Goose Game pattern the sources support is a different trade: the list names the outcome, the NPCs show their attention, the world carries the consequences, and the player supplies the plan.

The most useful next step is cheap. Ship the hint ladder and outcome-only wording on two or three existing stages (Map and Cone, Wet Floor, Jukebox Hero, where the systems already support emergent solutions) and have the same playtester try them. That test would reveal whether the missing feeling was the explicit hints or the lack of alternative solutions, and it would settle the open question this research could not answer from sources: how long players tolerate being stuck before the ladder should step in.
