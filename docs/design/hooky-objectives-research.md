# Give every Lokki to-do a victim

The trial puzzles P1 The Midsummer Buffet, P2 The Grand Opening and P3 Fire Drill do not hook because they are heists, not gags. Their 14 steps ask the player to remove a watcher ("The Chef should have somewhere else to be"), take an item, and carry it to a marker. No step names a funny picture with a person in it, and each level ends on a delivery to a spot where the wronged person is absent. Untitled Goose Game's lists work the other way. They name a specific, routine-bound person and an outcome that happens to them ("Make the groundskeeper wear his sun hat"), leave the method open, and in about half the items let the victim finish the joke with their own body. The research converges on why this hooks. The comedy is a benign violation of a pompous or rigid person's dignity. The open method gives a partial information gap, which is where curiosity peaks. The victim's reaction is the reward, and the level's memory is set by its peak and its end. Lokki already has most of the engine needed (craves and comfort stations, wants and yuck, the cleaner's pride, photographer clicks, tablecloth yank, contagion, slips, soaks, `send` and `witness` reactions), and P1 to P3 use almost none of it. The fix is mostly content: rewrite each to-do as "make this person suffer this indignity". Give each victim a visible trait that the trick and the joke share. Accept two or more ways, each with its own punchline, and end on the victim discovering what the gull did. A short run of engine work (escalating reactions, ritual countdowns, cross-wired NPC reactions, a quality test that requires a victim) makes the rule stick. **Evidence caveat:** almost every web page was blocked, so external claims come from search-result summaries. Only the Lokki repo was read directly.

## How much weight each claim can bear

Four grades are used. **Repo-verified** means the audit read the file on branch `m99-collide-carry` (it did not play the game). **Search summary** means a search engine relayed the claim and a URL is given, but the page was not opened. For Goose Game to-do wording, "snippet-confirmed" means the exact words appeared in at least one snippet. **Unverified** means it comes from background knowledge with no source found this session. **Inference** is reasoning by the researchers or by me, and is labelled as such. No external source was read in full. Fetches failed for the Goose Game wiki, Nintendo Life, Game Developer, PC Gamer, grumpygamer.com and ACMI. So even well-known quotes (Schafer's "nothing is funny 30 times in a row", the Hitchcock bomb) rest on summaries. The playtester's words, "the to-dos are not interesting and hook enough", were given by the user. They are not in the repo, and the M95 to M99 "Playtest notes" sections are empty ([docs/milestones/M99.md](/home/user/lokki/docs/milestones/M99.md)).

## Goose Game's to-dos are set-ups whose punchline is a person

House House describe their games as "physical comedy games" in the line of clowning, silent film and cartoons: "tricking people, stealing things, hiding in bushes and honking a lot" ([Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/untitled-goose-game-interview), search summary). They built content from "a large list of gags… mostly a bunch of slapstick cliches", and a developer summed up an item as "a set-up and a punchline" ([Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-honk-an-i-untitled-goose-game-i-q-a); [Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-house-house-s-i-untitled-goose-game-i-), search summaries). Disseldorp says part of the craft is noticing when the game is already doing something funny and then "completing the joke" ([VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/honks-vs-quacks-a-long-chat-with-the-developers-of-untitled-goose-game/), search summary). A GDC 2020 narrative review describes the core loop as "interrupting normalcy and examining the reactions of the NPCs" ([Arroz, GDC Vault](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/GameNarrativeReview/MaryArroz-UntitledGooseGame-paper.pdf), search summary).

The list itself bears this out. Snippet-confirmed items include "Get the groundskeeper wet", "Steal the groundskeeper's keys", "Rake in the lake", "Trap the boy in the phone booth", "Make the boy wear the wrong glasses", "Make someone buy back their own stuff", "Make the man spit out his tea", "Make the man go barefoot", "Make someone break the fancy vase", "Make someone prune the prize rose", "Make the old man fall on his bum" and "Drop a bucket on the burly man's head" ([GamesRadar walkthrough](https://www.gamesradar.com/untitled-goose-game-walkthrough-puzzle-guide/); [GameFAQs Pub](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/switch/246682-untitled-goose-game/faqs/77939/the-pub); [Fandom High Street](https://untitledgoosegame.fandom.com/wiki/High_Street), all search summaries). "Make the groundskeeper wear his sun hat" and "hammer his thumb" were seen in snippets, but sources disagree on the exact words. The researchers classified the roughly 29 main items from the described mechanics (inference, their own count). **About 75%** target a specific role's pride, routine or cherished possession. **About 45 to 50%** let the NPC perform the final action: he sits, spits, prunes, buys back, puts on the glasses, walks into the garage. **About 25 of 29** name the outcome rather than the method. The exceptions are mostly "goose self-expression" items (have a picnic, go shopping, set the table). Typical depth is two or three steps: one routine window plus one goose action.

Three craft patterns carry the weight. First, **the victim completes the joke**. The rose is pruned by its owner, who was tricked into trimming her topiary. The boy's toy plane is shelved by the shopkeeper and bought back by the boy ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/untitled-goose-game-complete-puzzle-guide/); [High Street wiki](https://untitledgoosegame.fandom.com/wiki/High_Street), search summaries). Second, **possessions are cross-wired between NPCs**. In the Back Gardens, the woman's vase moved to the man's side gets broken by him, his hat and pipe end up on her bust, and her bell makes him spit his tea. On the High Street, trapping the boy empties the TV shop ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/untitled-goose-game-walkthrough-puzzle-guide/), search summary). The researchers count about eight multi-NPC items (inference). Third, **the hidden and timed lists recycle the same systems** as escalations and callbacks. Examples are "Lock the groundskeeper out of the garden" after stealing his keys, and the old man's woolen hat after the stool gag ([HyperX secret lists](https://ag.hyperxgaming.com/article/8586/untitled-goose-game-secret-to-do-lists), search summary; wording partly unverified).

The items critics quote share one shape. Den of Geek singles out "locking a shopkeeper in their garage or stealing a kid's glasses" ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/us/games/283441/untitled-goose-game-review)). GameSpot points to a man who must "stumble around with a bucket on his head" ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/untitled-goose-game-review-saved-by-the-bell/1900-6417308/)). Game Developer leads with "soaking the gardener or making someone hammer their own thumb" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-honk-an-i-untitled-goose-game-i-q-a)). All three are search summaries. **Inference:** the favourites have a victim with a visible routine you watch first, a small deniable move by the goose, a victim who completes the gag with their own body, and a result readable from across the screen.

Other mischief games confirm the pattern by contrast (search summaries throughout). Goat Simulator 3's best-liked quests were the ones where "even the dullest side quests had a twist or a gag" ([Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/xbox-series-x-reviews/2022/11/17/goat-simulator-3-review-the-best-dumbest-fun/)), while its generic Instincts were "semi-moronic time wasters" ([GosuNoob](https://www.gosunoob.com/reviews/goat-simulator-3-review-bleaterminator/)). Little Kitty, Big City's fetch, break and steal tasks drew "Repetitive, But Oozes Charm" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/little-kitty-big-city)) and "lacking the character and humour of Untitled Goose Game" ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/little-kitty-big-city/review)). Hitman's Mission Stories start from an overheard want ("the target wants a drink"). IOI cut them roughly in half in Hitman 3, keeping those that "open up different areas of the map" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/hitman-3-interview-the-dna-of-an-io-game-and-what-it-means-for-the-future-of-hitman-and-bond/)), after critics complained of "excessive handholding" ([Wikipedia, Hitman 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitman_(2016_video_game))). Donut County wraps each level "in a complaint, and each complaint is its own tiny comic beat" ([GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/de/games/donut-county/review)). The repeated lesson is that a short task holds attention when it has a face and a reaction. It reads as a chore when it ends in a checkbox.

## Comedy and hook research explain why a victim and an open method matter

**Benign violation** is the best-supported account of what is funny. Laughter results from "violations that are simultaneously seen as benign", and violations become benign through weak commitment to the norm, psychological distance, or an alternative reading such as play ([McGraw and Warren 2010, Psychological Science](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/mcgraw.warren.2010.pdf), abstract and summaries). A 2021 integrative review kept the same recipe ([SPSP summary](https://www.spsp.org/news-center/blog/warren-barsky-mcgraw-humor-theory)). Bergson's "mechanical encrusted upon the living" ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/laughter-henri-bergson)) explains why a rigid routine is a loaded gag. Johnstone's status work ([Imprology](https://www.imprology.com/impro/status.shtml)) explains why lowering a pompous person is the safest laugh. Chuck Jones's Road Runner rule that "the coyote is always more humiliated than harmed" ([Open Culture](https://www.openculture.com/2015/03/chuck-jones-9-rules-for-drawing-road-runner-cartoons.html)) is the kindness guard; the attribution of the rules is disputed. Reviewers credit Goose Game's "resigned, irritated" villagers, who never harm the goose ([Edge via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20191107/281702616512754)). **Inference:** a Lokki victim should have status above the gull, a fixation or routine, harm only to dignity or property, and a quick, grumbling recovery. That rules out P1's ending, where the gull does Granny a favour. It also rules out P2's ribbon cut answered with hearts, which reads as approval rather than "poor Mayor".

**Agency changes who tells the joke.** An ACMI essay says Goose Game's comedy "comes from the doing, not the telling" ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/untitled-goose-player-comedians/), snippet only). IOI says testers chose Hitman kills because the act itself was darkly funny ([Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/v2/hitman-dev-says-humor-a-vital-part-of-the-series/)). Schafer warned that "nothing is funny 30 times in a row" ([AV Club, GDC 2010 panel](https://avclub.com/avc-at-gdc-10-the-comedy-in-games-panel-1798219354)). Hitchcock's bomb-under-the-table contrast of suspense and surprise ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/alfred-hitchcock-and-francois-truffaut-explain-surprise-vs-suspense)) maps onto the player's position (inference): the gull is the bomb, the victim is oblivious, and a visible clock (a selfie countdown, a ferry horn) supplies the suspense. The rule of three (set, repeat, break) is standard comedy-writing craft ([No Film School on Vorhaus](https://nofilmschool.com/john-vorhaus-comedy-writing-tools)). Gilbert's rubber chicken shows that absurd solutions stay fair when the item and the rule are visible first ([Thimbleweed Park blog](https://blog.thimbleweedpark.com/design_dilemma1.html)).

The **hook** evidence is stronger on mechanism than on games. A 2025 meta-analysis of 59 publications found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks (Zeigarnik not supported). It did find a **resumption rate of about 67% against a 50% baseline** (Ovsiankina supported), with participant involvement as a moderator ([Ghibellini and Meier 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)). An open loop pulls only if the player already cares. Curiosity peaks at **intermediate confidence** (an inverted U), so a total blank is weak and a partial gap is strong ([Kang et al. 2009](https://www.emdgroup.com/en/stories/colin-camerer.html); [2023 replication](https://opendata.uni-halle.de/bitstream/1981185920/117064/1/s00426-023-01841-9.pdf)). Loewenstein notes that curiosity tends to **disappoint once satisfied** ([abstract](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)). The payoff therefore has to over-deliver. The **goal gradient** speeds effort as the goal nears, and illusory head-starts work: a 12-stamp card with 2 free stamps was finished in a median of 10 days against 15 ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/chazen-global-insights/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration)). The **peak-end** effect shapes how players remember casual games ([Gutwin et al., CHI 2016](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~jeromew/data/COMP766/CHI2016/p5608-gutwin.pdf)). Self-determination research links game enjoyment to autonomy and competence ([Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski 2006](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/7228/)). Hodent warns that a game can have clear goals "yet [be] boring" ([EGD summary](https://elitegamedevelopers.substack.com/p/egd-084-celia-hodent-game-ux)). **Inference:** clarity, which is what M94 to M99 improved, is necessary but not sufficient. The hook comes from caring about a specific person (involvement), an outcome whose method is half-known (curiosity), a reaction that varies (reward), and a level that ends on its biggest beat (peak-end). No game-specific study of to-do wording was found.

## P1 to P3 removed the joke when they removed the recipe

The repo audit makes the diagnosis concrete (repo-verified unless marked). A grep for `joke` over [buffet.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/buffet.ts) and [puzzles2.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/puzzles2.ts) finds nothing. Yet the author's own rule says an objective is unfinished unless it has a joke "with a person in it" and "two reactions from two different sources, one of them a witness" ([docs/design/puzzle-quality.md](/home/user/lokki/docs/design/puzzle-quality.md)). Every Act 1 and Act 2 stage chain carries such a card. The audit's classification of the 14 trial steps (its own count) finds **6 steps that only move a watcher away** (chef, maître, stall, guard, monger, and in effect the Mayor), **4 grabs** and **4 terminal deliveries** (blanket, ribbon, reporter, muster). Only one outcome line comes close to a funny picture: "The ribbon should be cut, with everyone watching."

| Level | What the note asks | Where the comedy actually is | What the player sees at the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Buffet | Chef "should have somewhere else to be"; Maître "should stop pacing"; take the pie; pie "belongs on the red-checked picnic blanket" | The slip and the bell exist only as a means to clear watchers | Heart emotes at Granny's blanket and "The pie is home. Nobody knows how." The Chef never finds his table empty |
| P2 Opening | A crowd of four at the ribbon; the Mayor loses his scissors; ribbon cut "with everyone watching"; first cone; cone to the Reporter | The Mayor's ceremony is upstaged, but only by an anger emote | Hearts at the ribbon, then a laugh at a Reporter with "no behaviour at all" (`stationary, post`, role `'reporter'`) |
| P3 Fire Drill | Stall has no customers; the Guard is off his round; the Fishmonger looks elsewhere; take the fish; fish at the MUSTER sign | The false drill, the soak and the slip are blindfolds; no reaction is defined for `slipped:guard` or `soaked:fishmonger` | "A golden fish at the assembly point. Nobody saw anything." |

The cast cannot carry jokes, because every member shares one behaviour: `watch`, which means mind a prop and chase the gull if it is seen. None has `craves`, `wants`, `routine`, `relentless` or a role-coded trait such as `cleaner`, `selfie` or `blogger`. Only the Maître and the Guard move, each between two points. M96 made the cast readable only by costume (hats and name tags). **Inference:** in Goose Game the puzzle is "what does this person do when…?" and the answer lies in their character. In P1 to P3 the answer is the same for everyone (break line of sight), so learning one watcher teaches nothing about the next. The motives are also incoherent: Granny guards the pie that is supposedly for her picnic, the fish goes to a muster sign for no reason, and the cone goes to a reporter who does nothing with it. The M99 fix removed the impossible "get away" tails ([M99.md](/home/user/lokki/docs/milestones/M99.md)), but the deliveries before them are the same padding: a marker with no person reacting.

The audit also shows the comparison that matters. Stage chains in [arc1.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/arc1.ts) and [arc2.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/arc2.ts) hang trick and joke on the same trait. In Bun voyage, "A dockhand so proud of his spotless quay storms off to scold a bun left on it". In The Clean Freak, "A vendor so proud of his sign drops everything to scrub every splat". In Map and Cone, "He fumes, follows, buys, beams, and then fumes again". Their weakness was method-heavy hints and wait steps, which the earlier research targeted and M94 to M95 addressed with outcome wording, several events per step and watcher gauges. **Inference:** the trials fixed "work it out" and lost "who and why it's funny" along the way. The stages had the opposite balance. The playtester's complaint fits the second gap. A smaller cause also plays a part. M95's several ways per step vary the tactics for blinding someone, not the funny result, so each experiment produces the same outcome.

The engine already holds what the rewrites need (repo-verified definitions; unused in P1 to P3 unless noted). The NPC traits are `craves` with `stations` (angry, then follows the taunt, buys comfort food, and chases again after `hadComfort`); `wants` (eats things nearby; salmiakki or herring triggers `yuck`); `routine` and `stance` (photo, watch, rest); `relentless`; the cleaner's `signAt`, `cleanStep`, `signSplat` and `cleanerRobbed`; and the selfie and blogger `selfieClick` with `inFrame` and `who`. The switch effects are `yank` with `goods`, `domino` and `disguise`, plus the `summon`, `dance`, `alarm` and `soak` already used. The events are `reclaimed`, `startled` with `dropped`, `spread` (contagion), `knockdown`, `boo`, `review` and `bought`. The reaction fields are `send` to any named goal, `distract`, `hunt`, `emit` and `witness` with any emote. The unused emotes include `question`, `point`, `facepalm`, `zzz`, `star` and `sweat`. Level options include `secret` hidden to-dos and `coopRequired`. Two limits matter for the rewrites. The `selfieStep` and `cleanStep` behaviours are keyed to role strings, so a Reporter or Fishmonger needs a role change or a role-agnostic trait flag (inference from the code shape). The `yank` goods type accepts only `'bun' | 'icecream'` ([types.ts](/home/user/lokki/src/data/levels/types.ts)). Whether the P series gates any verbs in `src/gameplay/mechanics.ts` was not checked.

## What changes in understanding

The earlier diagnosis, "Lokki hands over answers instead of questions", was right but incomplete. Hiding the method raises curiosity only if the player already wants the outcome, and the meta-analysis's involvement moderator and Hodent's "clear yet boring" warning point to the same missing piece. P1 to P3 prove the point: they hide the method well and still fail because the outcome is "a pie on a blanket". The unit of design should be the victim's trait, not the item. One trait does three jobs at once: it is the lever (the trick), the joke (the indignity) and the reason to care (a person the player wants to annoy). When those three come apart, as with "make the Maître stop pacing", the step becomes logistics however well it is hinted.

A second implication is that much of the needed variety is already in the engine as reactions waiting to be attached to victims. That makes the rewrite cheap to test. Rebuild one trial (P2 is the closest) per the lists below, using only existing systems, and ask the same playtester whether they wanted to see what the Mayor does next. If they do, the engine work below becomes worth building. If they don't, the problem lies in the payoff staging (camera, animation, timing), which these sources cannot settle.

## (a) Rules for writing a Lokki to-do

These rules are inferences that synthesise the sources above. The examples are graded as follows: **Goose** examples come from search summaries, **Lokki** examples are repo-verified.

| # | Rule | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Name a person and what happens to them.** Use "Make the <role> <indignity>" or an outcome on a named victim, with no method, key or marker in the note. | Goose: "Make the man spit out his tea". Lokki (P2): "The ribbon should be cut, with everyone watching." | Lokki (P3): "The fish should be at the green MUSTER sign." |
| 2 | **Never put the obstacle on the list.** Clearing a watcher is a means. If a step only blinds someone, fold it into the gag or make the clearing itself the gag. | Goose: the phone booth trap both humiliates the boy and empties the TV shop. Lokki rewrite: "Make the Chef answer the bell for nobody." | Lokki (P1): "The Chef (white hat) should have somewhere else to be." Lokki (P3): "The Fishmonger should be looking at something else." |
| 3 | **Hang it on a visible trait or routine that is both the lever and the joke.** Show the routine at least twice before it matters. | Goose: the old man's harmonica stool. Lokki (1.1): "A dockhand so proud of his spotless quay storms off to scold a bun left on it." | Lokki (P1): "The Maître (top hat, pacing) should stop pacing." The pacing means nothing. |
| 4 | **Let the victim finish the joke with their own body** (sits, eats, buys, slips, scrubs, poses). | Goose: "Make someone prune the prize rose"; "Make someone buy back their own stuff". | Lokki (P2): the cone set down beside a static Reporter, who does nothing. |
| 5 | **Punch up and keep it benign.** Hit pride, schedules and property, never bodies. Choose pompous or fussy targets with a quick, grumbling recovery. Children and the frail get good-bird to-dos framed as such. | Goose: the groundskeeper's sun hat. Lokki (1.5): the Warden "swings his umbrella at a gull that is not there, and ends up pooped on". | Lokki (P1): the finale is a favour to Granny, with hearts and no victim, yet it is presented as mischief. |
| 6 | **Make the reaction the wrong emotion for the victim and the right one for the crowd.** The victim shows question, anger or facepalm; the witnesses laugh or point. Hearts are for good-bird to-dos only. | Lokki (1.1): "My spotless quay!" plus a laugh witness. | Lokki (P2): `ribbonCut` gives hearts in a 16 m radius and a delighted vendor. |
| 7 | **Two or more ways, each with its own punchline,** and all of them count. | Goose: the wrong glasses can be swapped, dropped after a trip, or left for him to pick up. | Lokki (P3): three ways to make the Fishmonger look away, all ending in the same nothing. |
| 8 | **End the level on its biggest reaction, where the victim discovers the loss.** No delivery to an empty marker after the peak. | Goose: the bucket on the burly man's head closes the Pub. | Lokki (P1 to P3): every level ends on a carry to a blanket, a reporter or a sign. |
| 9 | **Escalate one victim in threes, and save a callback for the hidden list.** | Goose: steal the keys, then (As Well) lock him out. | Lokki (P3): three different watchers, each blinded once, with no escalation. |
| 10 | **Hide one item as a partial gap, not a blank.** Half-reveal it as "??? …the Reporter's front page" once the player is near. | Goose: the secret "To Do (As Well)" list. | A bare "???" with no hint, which sits in the low-curiosity tail. |
| 11 | **Write in an innocent chore-list voice.** Keep it short, playful and euphemistic. | Goose: "Do the washing"; "Help the woman dress up the bust". | Lokki (P2): "A crowd (four or more) should be waiting by the red ribbon, expecting something." This is a staging instruction. |

## (b) Rewritten to-do lists for P1 to P3

These are design proposals (inference). Each line names the existing engine hooks it relies on (repo-verified as existing), and anything marked **[new]** needs work from list (c). Each level has three main to-dos ordered so the biggest gag comes last, plus one hidden to-do. The P2 co-op objective uses the existing `coopRequired`.

### P1 The Midsummer Buffet: the Chef's golden pie

Premise shift: Granny is no longer the pie's guardian. She is a sweet-toothed picnicker at the next blanket, and the Chef is a proud man who cannot ignore his service bell.

| To-do (note text) | Victim | Trait or routine | Punchline the player sees | Ways to cause it (any counts) | Engine hooks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Make the Chef answer the bell for nobody (three times).** | Chef | He answers every ring at once, every time (a Bergson routine, rule of three) | Ring 1: he arrives at an empty bell (`question`). Ring 2: `anger` ("Who keeps ringing?"). Ring 3: he slams the bell down and sulks at it (`facepalm`); guests `laugh` | Peck the bell; drop a bottle on it or poop on it **[new: switches fire on poop or toss hits]**; in co-op, one gull rings while the other hides | `summon` switch, `say`, `witness`; per-count reactions **[new: escalating reactions]** |
| **Make the Maître lose his dignity in front of the guests.** | Maître | Pompous patrol that ends with straightening the tablecloth at each end | Any of three: he slips on a splat mid-strut; he is buried in flying buns when the cloth is yanked; he breaks into a dance to the jukebox. Each time the guests `laugh` and `point` | Poop on his patrol line (`slipped:maitre`); peck the tablecloth corner (`yank`, goods: buns); peck the jukebox while he passes (`danced`) | `slipped`, `yank` switch, `dance` switch, `witness` (laugh, point) |

| ??? *Give Granny a nasty surprise.* (hidden; reveals as "…Granny's sweet tooth") | Granny | Same sweet tooth | She bites salmiakki, makes a face (`yuck`, `facepalm`) and offers it to the Chef | Drop salmiakki by her blanket; swap it for the pie on her blanket | `wants` plus salmiakki, then `yuck`; `secret: true` |

### P2 The Grand Opening: the Mayor's big day

Premise shift: this is the Mayor's moment, and the Reporter is a photographer with a countdown. Every to-do lowers the same pompous man, three times over.

| To-do (note text) | Victim | Trait or routine | Punchline the player sees | Ways to cause it (any counts) | Engine hooks |
